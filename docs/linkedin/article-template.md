## B. Publish-ready article

### Title

[Use a number, tension or decision question; avoid a generic topic label.]

### Standfirst

[One or two sentences that state why the decision matters now and identify the
specific tension. Do not spend the opening defining the whole field.]

### 01 [First evidence-led section]

Open with the thesis. Establish the first one or two evidence anchors and label
their evidence class in plain language. Use exact denominators, dates, geography
and population when they affect interpretation.

### 02 [Mechanism, segment or competitive interpretation]

Connect the evidence to the decision question. State clearly when the argument
uses adjacent-disease extrapolation or analyst inference. The article should
explain the reasoning, not reproduce the complete deck table.

### Exhibit 1 — [Decision-useful caption]

[Insert one legible public-use figure or compact exhibit. The caption must say
what is measured, the population/date and the evidence class. Source line and
ledger rows are mandatory.]

### 03 [Negative evidence or counterargument]

Present the strongest material uncertainty or counterargument. Use the controlled
states not found, not reported, not studied, not evaluable and negative
correctly. Do not turn missing evidence into a zero or an unfavorable result.

### 04 [Decision implication]

State what the evidence changes for a portfolio, clinical-development or market
decision. Keep clinical and commercial differentiation separate. Avoid medical
advice, investment advice and false precision.

### What the full deck adds

[Name the exact mapped session and the additional matrix, segmentation, model,
sensitivity, gate scoring or method it contains. Link only to a validated deck
destination and state the access method accurately.]

### References

List the public sources needed for the article. The full claim-level apparatus
remains in the deck and Evidence Ledger, but the article must provide enough
source information for a reader to verify its material claims.
